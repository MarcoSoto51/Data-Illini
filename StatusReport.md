## Changes to Project Plan
Since Milestone 2, we have refined our project plan to improve clarity, feasibility, and alignment with our research objectives. In particular, we more clearly defined the datasets selected for analysis by specifying their scope, structure, and relevance. We also expanded our explanation of how these datasets will be integrated by outlining more concrete steps for merging, including the key variables used to link them and potential challenges related to compatibility and data quality. Additionally, we strengthened our discussion of potential data gaps and limitations by offering a more thorough assessment of missing or incomplete information and its possible impact on our analysis. These updates result in a more detailed and realistic project plan that better positions us for the next stages of data processing and analysis.

## Challenges Encountered
One challenge we faced was making sure the project scope was realistic. Early in our planning project, the planning process, the project direction was less focused, so it was not really clear how the datasets would actually connect or if the work we had to do would be manageable within the time we had. We covered this by revising the project plan so that it can clearly center on the FARS auxiliary datasets and explain how they relate to each other.

Another big challenge was clearly understanding the structure of the datasets well enough to fully explain the integration process. Because the project involves accident-level, person-level, and vehicle-level data, we were forced to think carefully about how those different levels of information would fit together. This was addressed by looking over the dataset descriptions more closely and identifying ST_CASE as the main variable that we will use to connect the dataset.

In addition, we know that missing values, coded fields, and inconsistent reporting could affect our analysis later. Because of this, we had to make sure that the updated project plan includes a clearer discussion of these issues and how they could affect the project. Even though we have not begun coding yet, identifying these problems now allows us to get a better starting point for the implementation phase.
